OPT Employer Checker

Free, unofficial. Not affiliated with DHS or USCIS.

A free Chrome extension that shows E-Verify and H-1B information next to employers on LinkedIn job pages, so F-1 and STEM OPT job seekers can spot problems before they accept an offer.

Please read: this tool cannot guarantee that an employer will sign an I-983, and it is not legal advice. Your DSO is the authority. Ask your DSO.

What you see on LinkedIn

Two small chips appear next to the employer name. These are the real chips, so you know what to look for.

E-Verify chip

E-Verify: enrolled
The employer has an open E-Verify enrollment on file. Click the chip to see when it started and whether an end date is listed.
E-Verify: ended
We only have enrollments that have ended. No current one is in our data, but one could be missing.
E-Verify: not found
The name was not in our data. "Not found" is not a verdict. It does not mean the employer is not enrolled. Names must match exactly after normalizing, so a similar-looking name is never shown as a match. Ask the employer for their E-Verify company ID.
E-Verify: couldn’t check
The data could not be loaded, for example because you are offline. It never means "not enrolled". Try again.

H-1B chip

H-1B: 12 · FY2025
12 H-1B petitions were approved for this employer in fiscal year 2025, the latest full year, from the USCIS H-1B Employer Data Hub. Click the chip for the last few years.
H-1B: 8 · FY2026*
The star means the year is still in progress, so the number is partial and will grow.
H-1B: 0 · FY2025
The employer appears in the data, but with no approved petitions that year.
H-1B: none found
No H-1B records under this exact name. Spelling variants are not merged.

On crowded LinkedIn lists the chips are shorter (for example "E-Verify" and "H-1B 12") so they fit on the card. Open the chip for the full wording.

Data and sources

  • E-Verify: the public E-Verify employer search data. We cover most E-Verify employers, so an employer can be missing.
  • H-1B approvals: the USCIS H-1B Employer Data Hub.
  • Data date: loading…. The extension always shows the data date next to what it tells you.
  • Some E-Verify enrollments cover only federal-contract work. An enrollment on file may not apply to the job you are applying for.

Privacy

  • The extension reads the company name shown on the LinkedIn job page you are viewing, inside your browser. It only runs on linkedin.com.
  • Company names never leave your browser. The name is normalized and hashed on your computer, and only the first 3 characters of the hash are used to request a data file. That file is shared by hundreds of employers, so the data site cannot tell which employer you looked at.
  • The data files are static files hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any website, GitHub can see your IP address and which data file was requested. We do not run a server, an account system, analytics, advertising or tracking, and we do not collect, sell or share personal data.
  • Downloaded data files are kept in your browser's own cache so lookups are fast. They contain public employer data only. Nothing about you or your searches is stored.
  • The extension asks for no browser permissions beyond reading LinkedIn pages and fetching these data files.

Questions to ask before you accept

The extension is a first look, not an answer. Before you accept an offer, confirm these with the employer, and with your DSO:

  • Is the employer enrolled in E-Verify right now? Ask for their E-Verify company ID.
  • Will they sign the I-983 training plan?
  • Who will supervise your training, and are they at the employer itself?
  • Do they employ you directly, or are you placed at a client by a staffing or consulting firm?
  • Does your DSO agree this job and employer meet the OPT rules?
